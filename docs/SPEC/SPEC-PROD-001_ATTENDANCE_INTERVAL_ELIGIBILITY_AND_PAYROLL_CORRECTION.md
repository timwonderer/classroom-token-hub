## I. Purpose

Define reproducible interval eligibility, original-settlement provenance, and bounded compensation without rewriting attendance or financial history.

## II. Scope

Completed attendance intervals within one class and target seat; payroll settlement, interval invalidation, and residual correction. This documentation authorizes a future implementation. It introduces no runtime code, migration, historical backfill, or compatibility bridge.

## III. Authority Level

This technical contract binds only through explicit incorporation by `DOM-PROD-001` and `FEAT-PROD-003` / `FEAT-PROD-005`. It does not independently authorize mutations. `INV → DOM → FEAT` governs; higher-level conflicts stop implementation. Cross-domain composition belongs solely to the FEAT contracts; the owning domains neither import nor invoke one another.

## V. Interval Identity and Terminal Eligibility

PROD pairs one opening `active` event with its corresponding closing `inactive` event in its canonical ordered timeline. Identity is `(class_id, target_seat_id, opening_event_id, closing_event_id)`. Both rows belong to that class and target, form the completed pair selected by PROD, and satisfy canonical class-day boundaries. An arbitrary pair, an open interval, or an end preceding its start is ineligible for this operation. A legitimate zero-duration completed pair is allowed.

PROD derives credited seconds through canonical elapsed duration; invalidation changes only compensation eligibility. Original scans, credited duration evidence, and current attendance state are preserved and remain inspectable. Invalidation is terminal: it cannot be edited, reversed, deleted while its target exists, or converted into a new payable interval. Paid work remains historically settled, including after correction or reversal; it never re-enters unpaid work.

`attendance_interval_invalidation` is the append-only PROD-owned decision record. Required fields are `id`, `class_id`, `actor_seat_id`, `target_seat_id`, `opening_event_id`, `closing_event_id`, `recorded_at` (UTC), `reason_code`, `idempotency_key`, `correlation_id`, `receipt_json`, `lineage_event_id`, `lineage_token` (64 characters), and `lineage_version`. Business fields are frozen at INSERT. Audit linkage is opaque and excluded from the protected payload; all three fields initialize together exactly once in the creating transaction, whose commit requires matching complete creation evidence. Late attachment, replacement, partial linkage and missing evidence fail closed under DOM-PROD-001 §XI.4 and the Operations creation protocol. Reasons are exactly `INVALID_ATTENDANCE`, `NON_WORK_ACTIVITY`, or `DUPLICATE_PARTICIPATION`; no unrestricted notes are accepted or persisted. Unique interval identity prevents a second invalidation under any key. Command identity is `(class_id, FEAT-PROD-005, idempotency_key)`.

Source-event references are within PROD. `class_id` and seat references are shared anchors; other-domain internal rows are referenced only through opaque locators. The protected-row audit pointer is the narrowly mandated linkage of `INV-ARC-016`, not a general permission for cross-domain foreign keys.

## VI. Original Settlement Provenance and Cent Allocation

Each new payroll settlement freezes its complete interval membership in `payroll_event.summary_json`: opening/closing event IDs, credited seconds, governing `policy_uuid`, original per-minute `pay_rate`, and `allocation_version = 1` (including the version-1 quantization convention). Existing setting-share and scheduled-occurrence provenance remains present. References are scoped to the event's class and target. Policy UUID is an opaque non-FK locator; no subsequent rate lookup may reprice settled work.

Persist source inputs, not allocated amounts: no earnings cache, payroll amount column, or monetary amount in PROD summary JSON is introduced. PROD owns frozen participation, original pricing facts, settlement membership, and eligibility conclusions. FEAT supplies those proven inputs to Ledger; Ledger performs monetary allocation/correction arithmetic and validates it against its original posted credit. Ledger must never discover or reinterpret attendance intervals, select payroll policies, or resolve business eligibility itself.

For each governing setting, retain the original pricing rule: total credited seconds × original per-minute rate ÷ 60, quantized once to cents using exact decimal/rational arithmetic, nearest cent with ties to even (`ROUND_HALF_EVEN`) for allocation version 1. Do not round interval durations or separately quantize interval earnings. Historical reproduction must establish the original quantization contract rather than infer it from today's default.

Let a setting's quantized share be integer cents C and its interval seconds be s_i, summing to S. Allocate floor(C × s_i / S) cents to each interval, then distribute the remaining cents one each in descending order of fractional remainder. Resolve equal remainders by ascending closing-event UTC timestamp, then ascending opening-event ID, then ascending closing-event ID. This ordering is canonical, not query order. Exact rational arithmetic prevents floating-point ties. If S = 0, C must be zero and every allocation is zero; nonzero C with S = 0 is an integrity failure. Negative credited seconds or a negative original payroll credit fail integrity validation.

Allocation sums must equal each original setting share, and setting shares must sum to the original posted payroll credit. Corrections reuse the full original allocation, never reallocate cents among surviving intervals. A zero-cent interval may be invalidated with no Ledger effect or correction payroll row.

Historical payment correction is allowed only when PROD can prove exact original membership and frozen pricing inputs, and Ledger can prove the original credit, quantization, allocations, and compensation history. Existing lawful immutable evidence may establish these facts; proximity in time, matching totals, or current rates cannot. Missing provenance denies the whole paid invalidation action without committing eligibility alone. No historical row is rewritten, no command reservation or lineage is fabricated, and no compatibility bridge is authorized.

#### A. Complete original business graph

PROD owns a class/target-scoped graph connecting completed canonical pair identities to every original business contribution and original settlement boundary relevant to them. A contribution is an originally selected UTC subsegment of a canonical interval: an open-at-payroll fragment, a later clipped remainder, a completed whole-pair contribution, or a separately identified positive incident top-up loss component. A still-open canonical interval cannot be invalidated. Its lawful eventual closing event may postdate an original fragment payment; original visibility evidence must prove which sources were present or absent at each original execution, not pretend that the later close already existed. The independently proven eventual close and unique intervening source continuity establish that completed canonical pair without altering original selection; current timestamp proximity alone cannot map an old fragment to it. Preserve its full raw pair duration separately from the original writer's selected/truncated pricing duration; a repeated-active restart must follow the independently proven original selection rule, never today's inferred continuation.

Each contribution identifies the original PROD event and window, canonical opening/closing IDs, selected subsegment endpoints, original rule/writer assignment, original policy/share assignment, original creation/source visibility and complete original source-set evidence. Zero-value `payroll` events and every intervening `payroll` boundary remain in the ordered business timeline; they can change a later window even when absent from incident `corrected_event_ids`. They do not invent zero-cent Ledger credits. Manual credits/top-ups remain non-boundary events. PROD establishes historical membership; it never discovers membership from a monetary total or computes attributable cents.

The graph must be closed over all connected contributions, events, shares and original positive credits, including other intervals sharing an event or top-up. Independently proven original selection and settlement evidence must establish that no relevant fragment, remainder, top-up, credit or earlier boundary was omitted. A present bounded scan, matching current totals or the list of incident source IDs cannot attest this completeness. Cap exhaustion, ambiguous ordering, multiple equally plausible graphs, missing original visibility/writer/policy evidence or changed evidence during the read yields unavailable for the entire graph; never qualify the convenient complete-looking portion. Existing fixed invocation bounds in SPEC-PROD-002 §V cover the complete graph before any page slice; pagination cannot divide its proof.

PROD identifies same-domain source references; Ledger independently binds each positive original credit through its own immutable class/target/account/mechanism/actor and original-business linkage evidence. Opaque Ledger origin locators are stable and versioned, not randomized presentation tokens or internal cross-domain FKs. Every original credit belongs to exactly one proven originating business event; multiple positive credits require independently proven per-credit share partition, never a proportional split guessed from their amounts. Unrelated teacher manual credits are not attendance contributions. The 79 observed PROD-PAY-001 source references are candidates, not an allowlist or proof of unique top-up membership.

#### B. Ledger-owned allocation and exact conservation

Ledger receives immutable PROD-owned graph membership/rule inputs, Policies-owned original settings and Operations-owned canonical evidence through FEAT. It validates monetary identity and exact original pricing before deriving allocations. Preserve each writer's original selection, duration aggregation, whole-second truncation, arithmetic operation order, decimal context and per-window/per-setting quantization. In particular, floor/truncate of an aggregate is not replaced by the sum of individually truncated fragments. The original integer pricing seconds and newly derived exact duration weights are distinct quantities.

For an ordinary proven original credit, reproduce each original setting/window share's integer cents once under its independently assigned original descriptor. Within that share, group all selected subsegments belonging to the same canonical pair; the pair's weight is their proven exact elapsed duration, represented in integer microseconds or exact rational seconds. Reject duplicate edges and conflicting overlapping selection claims; grouping is not permission to count the same original subsegment twice. Do not independently round component durations or component earnings. Allocate that share's original integer cents proportionally to these weights by exact rational largest remainder. Equal remainders sort by ascending canonical closing UTC, opening-event ID, then closing-event ID, independent of input order. A zero-weight pair receives zero; positive share cents with zero total weight is an integrity/unavailable evidence failure, not an allocation to a guessed pair. Shares sum exactly to their original credit; pair allocations never change after another pair is invalidated.

For a proven positive PROD-PAY-001 incident top-up, preserve the original positive window deltas separately. The source descriptor at `ad9574334d72fd92cc93402e851ab0ebc22aaad9`, `app/services/payroll/corrections.py:build_class_correction_proposal`, computes each included legacy window's delta as the original quantized whole worked-seconds price minus the separately quantized replay-paid-seconds price; a window is included only when worked seconds exceed paid seconds and the delta is positive. Preserve the exact source operation order, rate selection, decimal context and quantization; do not price aggregate unpaid seconds at today's/latest rate or combine windows before rounding. Assigning this descriptor still requires independently proven per-record writer provenance. Original summary amounts are historical observations, not Ledger monetary authority; validate every window delta against the proven original credit. Do not execute the retired writer.

For each included original top-up window, independently prove the complete original worked and replay-paid selected subsegments and their unique canonical-pair assignment. Require each pair's uniquely proven replay-paid temporal subsegments to be a subset of its proven worked subsegments within that original window. Form their nonoverlapping temporal set difference before summing exact durations; the grouped duration is the pair's **loss weight**. Paid-outside-worked segments, duplicate edges, conflicting overlapping claims or ambiguity in the temporal set membership deny attribution even when scalar durations match. These are new read-only rational duration weights, not a claim of historically stored integer per-pair seconds. The original writer's whole-second worked and paid aggregates are replayed separately; do not require or manufacture equality to the sum of individually floored loss weights. Negative loss weight, conflicting overlap, ambiguous loss assignment, or a positive delta with zero total loss weight denies attribution. Zero loss weight receives zero. Apply largest remainder with the same canonical pair tie order to that window's proven positive delta. Sum its derived allocations exactly to the original window delta, and all original window deltas exactly to the positive top-up credit. If an original top-up total matches but its loss components cannot be proven, attribution remains unavailable.

A positive top-up is a new original credit, never negative compensation and never a reduction of an earlier credit's cap. One interval may legitimately have allocations on several original fragment/remainder/top-up credits. Recover all proven contributions once per origin; do not omit a legitimate top-up merely because it concerns seconds also described by an original fragment. Conversely, duplicate edges or splitting one pair into several components must not create extra rounding positions or count a credit cent twice. Require conservation independently for every original share/window and credit, and for the complete graph. No amount is added to PROD summary, receipt or attendance rows.

#### C. Prior compensation and later execution boundary

Ledger proves every original credit's complete prior compensation, including accepted pending and posted principal effects, under its unchanged creation/compensation contracts. Funding legs have zero attribution. Missing version-specific evidence, unsigned reversal linkage or absence of new columns cannot imply zero. A proven full reversal or complete residual recovery of an origin makes all its remaining interval contribution zero. Partial compensation requires uniquely proven attribution to this graph's canonical pair/contribution identities; a known aggregate recovered total does not identify which pair was compensated. Never subtract the same unknown partial recovery from every interval, cap an arbitrary interval at the aggregate remainder, or invent a proportional allocation. Ambiguous partial attribution denies the entire paid invalidation, even where a separate per-credit residual command can prove its aggregate remainder.

For each origin c, Ledger derives C_c original credit cents, A_i,c fixed allocation to interval i, and attributable prior interval recovery R_i,c. For qualified partial attribution, require 0 ≤ R_i,c ≤ A_i,c and complete conservation between attributed prior principal effects and the origin's proven total recovery. The interval's proposed recovery is the sum over all origins of A_i,c − R_i,c, except an origin proven fully recovered contributes zero. Each original credit also retains the independent cap 0 ≤ total accepted recovery ≤ C_c. A full/residual origin recovery absorbs all remaining contributions only when its complete provenance proves that disposition; no unrelated negative entry can do so. Graph completeness and original monetary creation/posting proof remain required even for zero-cent or fully recovered eligibility-only outcomes.

This documentation phase supplies a conditional pure calculation only. FEAT-PROD-005 later integration must be separately implemented/reviewed with all original credits locked in stable Ledger locator order after target seat and ClassEconomy, complete monetary-source locks, one command reservation and atomic business/audit/funding/recovery. Each origin has its own immutable recovery principal/intent and cap; no aggregate graph cap may hide an over-recovered origin. Full/residual/interval commands share that serialization. A missing origin denies all writes, rather than committing eligibility or the recoverable subset. Replay, signed preview, no correction fee, terminal eligibility and original cycle/Interpretation preservation remain unchanged. No writer or historical recovery is enabled by §VI.2.

## VII. Compensation, Replay, and Concurrency

An unpaid interval produces only its invalidation and audit record. A paid interval produces its invalidation, a `correction` payroll business event when money is recovered, Ledger compensation effects/reservation, and audit lineage atomically. Correction summary carries same-domain `original_payroll_event_id`, `correction_intent = INTERVAL_INVALIDATION`, invalidation ID, source-event IDs, and opaque `correction_intent_locator` and Ledger origin locator; no financial amount is stored there.

Ledger owns immutable `compensation_origin_locator`, nonnegative `compensation_amount_cents`, and `correction_intent_locator` on its monetary effects. Only actual recovery is attributable compensation; savings/checking transfer legs contribute zero. For each original credit, full reversal plus interval and residual compensation must not exceed that credit. Ledger validates and serializes the aggregate cap. PROD must not infer remaining recoverable money by adding payroll rows.

Exact full reversal is allowed only while the original credit is uncompensated. After partial compensation, `FEAT-PROD-003` uses a distinct residual `correction`: original credit minus all attributable prior compensation. Its summary has `correction_intent = RESIDUAL_RECOVERY` and original-event/intent provenance. It is neither a partial `REVERSAL` nor a `VOID`. Already fully recovered payments permit later eligibility invalidation with zero additional monetary recovery. Missing or inconsistent recovery history fails closed.

Corrections and reversals do not advance payroll windows, scheduled occurrences, payroll-cycle completion, or economic-cycle boundaries. Neither invalidation nor recovery rewrites completed Interpretation materializations.

Preview is a pure domain-query composition: interval eligibility, settlement evidence, authoritative proposed compensation, protection transfer, and resulting balances. Its identity covers every fact affecting confirmation. For a new command, serialize and revalidate the preview before mutation; any changed relevant fact returns `PREVIEW_CHANGED` without writes. Successful exact replay resolves the existing command and compares the original supplied request fingerprint before current-preview evaluation; it returns the original result even though the action itself changed eligibility or balances. Same key with changed actor, target, pair, reason, or preview identity returns `REPLAY_MISMATCH`. A different key for an already-invalidated pair returns `ALREADY_INVALIDATED` without recovery.

The immutable protected `receipt_json` stores `expected_preview_identity`, `fingerprint_version`, `canonical_intent_digest`, `original_settlement_disposition`, and `opaque_outcome_locators`. It contains no monetary amounts, personal notes, or cached earnings. It permits reconstruction of the original accepted result through owning-domain queries without evaluating today's eligibility or balances. New unpaid/zero-value commands use the invalidation record and receipt as the durable PROD result; monetary commands also follow Ledger reservation identity `(class_id, feat_code, idempotency_key)` under SPEC-LED-002. Do not expand that namespace by target, account, amount, or effect type.

Payroll settlement and invalidation share serialization for `(class_id, target_seat_id)` before eligibility selection. If invalidation commits first, payroll excludes the interval; if payroll commits first, invalidation observes paid membership and requires a refreshed paid preview before compensation. Reversal and all corrections also serialize against the original Ledger credit and current account settlement. Every required business row, monetary effect, reservation, and audit emission commits together or rolls back together.

## VIII. Funding and Lifecycle

For a correction debit, Ledger resolves the shared protection rule using Class Configuration's supplied current banking directive: when checking would be negative, transfer the entire shortfall from savings only if protection is enabled and savings can cover it completely. Otherwise savings remains untouched and checking takes the full debit, including below zero. Own-account transfers retain their separate sufficient-funds rule. Corrections incur no NSF fee, obligation, or deferred deduction.

Target-seat destruction removes its invalidations and source business records under existing lifecycle authority; class destruction removes all class-scoped records. Removing a provenance actor does not authorize removal of another surviving target's records. Lifecycle destruction is the sole deletion exception, not an invalidation operation. Audit handling follows its existing immutable-chain and lawful-destruction contracts; do not invent a retained business archive or new audit deletion path.
