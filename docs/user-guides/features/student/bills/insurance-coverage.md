# Insurance Coverage

## Overview

Insurance trades a small, repeating payment for protection against a large one-off loss. You pay a **premium** every period whether or not anything goes wrong; in exchange, when something does go wrong you can file a claim and get part of the money back.

Three things are worth knowing before you spend anything:

- Buying, paying a premium, and cancelling all ask for your **passphrase**.
- Your coverage only works while your premiums are paid. An unpaid premium **pauses** your coverage.
- Cancelling **does not refund you**.

## Step-by-step instructions

### Finding the page

Insurance has its own **Insurance** item in the sidebar. It is independent of Rent: turning Rent off does not hide Insurance, and turning Insurance off does not hide Rent. If your teacher has disabled Insurance, the item is not shown — unless you still owe a premium. Then it stays, the page reads *Insurance is closed to new purchases in this class*, and you can still pay what you owe.

### Reading the page

The page has three parts, top to bottom:

1. **Your coverage** — the policies you currently hold, each with the premium, *Since {date}*, and a badge: **Active** when your premiums are paid, or **Premium overdue** when one is not. If you hold none, it reads *You have no active insurance coverage.*
2. **Available policies** — everything you could buy.
3. **Your claims** — a read-only table of what you have filed, appearing only once you have filed something.

Each coverage card has **View policy**, **File a claim**, and **Cancel coverage**.

### Tier groups: pick one, not all

Most policies are offered in **tier groups**. A group is one card with several rows inside it, labelled **Basic**, **Mid**, and **Premium** — cheaper tiers pay back less, expensive ones pay back more.

**You may hold only one tier per group.** The group header tells you where you stand: *Choose one tier* before you buy anything, and a green **Enrolled — one plan per group** badge once you own a tier in it.

Each row shows the price and the terms on one line, like:

> $3.00 / weekly · reimburses 60% · up to 4× payout

Read that as: three dollars every week; an approved claim pays back 60% of what you lost; and across one coverage period the policy will not pay out more than four times the premium in total.

The button on the right tells you what you can do:

| Button | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Buy — $X** | Available. One click buys it. |
| **Your plan** | You already hold this tier. |
| **Unavailable** | You hold a *different* tier in this group. Cancel that one first. |

Some policies are not in a group at all and appear as standalone cards further down, with a fuller description and a **Buy — $X now** button.

### Buying

Type your **passphrase** into the box on the policy row, then press **Buy — $X**. Insurance is a money decision, so it is held to the same standard as a store purchase: the premium only leaves your account after the passphrase is verified. Your PIN does not work here.

If it works you get *Insurance purchased — first premium of $X paid.* The first premium comes out of **checking** immediately, and your first period of coverage starts the moment the purchase lands.

If it does not work, the message says why:

| Message | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| *Enter your passphrase to confirm the insurance purchase.* | The box was empty or the passphrase did not match. Nothing was charged. |
| *You don't have enough in checking to pay the first premium.* | Move money from savings, or wait for payday. |
| *You already hold a plan in this tier group. Cancel it first to switch tiers.* | One tier per group. |
| *You already hold active coverage for this policy.* | Nothing to do. |
| *That policy is no longer available for new coverage.* | Your teacher retired it. Existing holders keep theirs. |

### How your periods work

Your coverage runs in periods that start from the day you bought it:

- **Weekly** — every 7 days from your purchase day.
- **Monthly** — the same date each month as your purchase day. If a month is too short for that date, the period starts on the 1st of the next month instead. So coverage bought on January 31 renews on March 1, then March 31, then May 1.

Each new period starts at midnight. Every period costs the same premium you agreed to when you bought, no matter how long the month is.

### Paying premiums

A few days **before** each new period starts, that period's premium is billed and taken from checking automatically. You do not have to do anything if checking has enough money.

If checking is short, the premium stays unpaid:

- When the new period starts with the premium still unpaid, your coverage is **paused**. Your coverage card shows **Premium overdue**, and claims are refused until you pay.
- To pay, open **View policy**, enter your **passphrase**, and press **Pay overdue premium**. It pays your oldest unpaid premium first.
- Paying brings your coverage back **from that moment on**. It does not cover anything that happened while your coverage was paused.

What happens if you never pay depends on the policy. Your teacher chose one of these:

- **Keep billing** — the policy keeps billing you each period, and coverage stays paused until **every** unpaid premium is paid. Paying only the newest one does not bring it back.
- **Cancel after a number of days** — if the premium is still unpaid that many days after your coverage paused, your coverage ends. You still owe the premiums for periods that had already started, and paying them later does not bring the coverage back. To be covered again you buy a new policy.

### Cancelling

**Cancel coverage** sits on the card in **Your coverage**. It asks for your **passphrase** and then to confirm:

> Cancel this coverage? It will stop renewing — your benefits continue until the end of the current period.

Cancelling means **stop charging me for future periods**. It is not a refund and not an instant switch-off:

- The premium you already paid stays paid.
- Your coverage stays live, and you can still file claims against it, until the current period runs out.
- If next period's premium was already billed but you have **not paid any of it**, that bill is cancelled and your coverage ends when the current period does.
- If you **already paid** next period's premium, that period is yours: your coverage runs to the end of it. If you paid only **part** of it, the period still happens and you still owe the rest — your coverage stays paused until you pay it.
- Only then does it drop off **Your coverage**.

You get *Coverage cancelled — it won't renew.* If you had already cancelled, it says *This coverage is already set to not renew.* If the passphrase is missing or wrong you get *Enter your passphrase to confirm the cancellation.* and nothing changes.

### Switching tiers

There is no upgrade button. To move from **Basic** to **Premium** in the same group you cancel Basic, wait for your coverage to end, and then buy Premium. Until then, the other tiers in that group stay greyed out as **Unavailable**.

## Important notes

> [!WARNING]
> **A premium is a recurring charge, not a one-off.** After the first one, the policy bills you again every period until you cancel. Read the premium and the frequency on the row before you buy, and keep enough in checking for it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **An unpaid premium pauses your coverage.** A claim for something that happens while your coverage is paused is refused, and paying later does not change that. Keeping checking topped up before each period starts is what keeps you covered.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Cancelling gets you nothing back.** There is no refund, no partial credit, and no early exit. If you buy a weekly policy and cancel an hour later, you have paid for that week. Cancelling only saves you future premiums.

> [!NOTE]
> **Plan the tier switch around your periods.** Cancelling Basic partway through a period leaves you unable to buy Premium in that group until the period is out — and unable to buy Basic back either, since it is cancelled. Switch near the end of a period, not the middle.

> [!NOTE]
> **Coverage usually starts immediately.** You can file a claim against something that happens right after you buy, as long as the incident is *after* your purchase. Claims about things that happened before you bought the policy are rejected. The exception is a policy with a **waiting period** — any type of policy can have one. It becomes claimable a set number of days after you buy, and switching tiers restarts that wait because it is a new purchase.

> [!TIP]
> Insurance is only worth buying if the loss it covers is one you actually risk. Compare the premium across a whole term against the size of the thing it protects, and remember the *up to N× payout* ceiling caps what you can recover in a period no matter how much you lost.
