# PRN-PHL-002: Classroom Token Hub Open-Source Philosophy

## Purpose and authority

Classroom Token Hub is an open-source project. Its licensing model is noncommercial open
source, grounded in transparency, educational access, and respect for freely contributed labor.
This document explains that philosophy in CTH's own terms.

This document is **not a license**. It grants no permissions, creates no enforceable commercial
obligations, and does not amend the published license or any runtime contract. Project values,
current license text, and proposed commercial conditions are distinct. The
[published license](https://github.com/timwonderer/classroom-token-hub/blob/main/LICENSE)
defines the baseline legal terms; the
[commercial policy draft](../../user-guides/legal/commercial.md) records the intended services
permission and its unresolved legal implementation.

## Software that serves classrooms

CTH began as a teacher's personal response to commercial educational software that did not
adequately meet his classroom's needs. It exists to give educators a useful, inspectable
classroom economy platform without turning its functionality into a product for sale.

Its success is measured by whether educators have access to better software. If independent
projects, including commercial competitors, eventually serve those needs so well that CTH is
unnecessary, that is compatible with its purpose. Continued market relevance is not the goal.

The broader educational and design history is recorded in
[PRN-PHL-001](PRN-PHL-001_Design_Principles_And_Project_History.md).

## What open source means to CTH

CTH's conception of open source rests on five commitments:

- **Source availability:** people can inspect the implementation behind the platform.
- **Transparency:** design assumptions and decisions can be examined and questioned.
- **Educational accessibility:** educators should not have to buy software feature entitlements to use the classroom system.
- **Study and modification:** people can learn from and adapt the software within its published license permissions.
- **Respect for contributed labor:** sharing development work freely should not require granting others unrestricted rights to sell that work.

For CTH, these commitments belong together. Noncommercial restrictions express the project's
ethics. The project defines and publishes its permissions, obligations, and restrictions so
that readers can assess the actual terms instead of inferring rights from a label.

CTH's philosophical legitimacy does not depend on recognition or certification by another
organization. Its use of “open source” states its own framework; it is not a representation
that commercial rights are unrestricted.

## Commercial services have a place

**Commercial providers may sell services around CTH. They may not sell CTH itself.** This is
the project's intended commercial principle; the legal mechanism implementing it remains
subject to review.

Hosting, deployment, backups, operations, and support require real labor and infrastructure.
Providers should be able to earn reasonable fees, including profit, for doing that work.
CTH's freely distributed features should not become premium software entitlements because
someone else operates the server.

“Hosting with a free side of CTH” describes the intended arrangement. “CTH with a side of
hosting” describes the arrangement the policy excludes. Invoice wording cannot substitute
for examining what payment actually buys or controls.

The intended permission is automatic for providers who meet the published conditions.
Individual written approval is not the desired model. Contributions of fixes and improvements
are welcome but voluntary. Commercial permission should not depend on submission deadlines,
upstream acceptance, or the maintainer monitoring whether providers contribute.

## Independent work and competition

CTH does not claim exclusive control of classroom economy ideas. Other developers may study
its design principles and independently build economic engines or competing platforms,
including commercial ones. Their projects and licensing choices remain their own.

The intended restriction concerns commercialization of CTH's protected implementation.
An independent implementation and a copy or adaptation of CTH code are different questions.
As a legal reference, the U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes ideas, systems, and methods
from their protected expression; that distinction does not decide whether any particular
implementation copies protected material.
[Copyright Office guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html).

CTH's principles do not require other independent developers to share its philosophy.

## Different philosophies of software freedom

The Open Source Initiative's framework includes unrestricted commercial use. CTH's framework
places noncommercial stewardship and protection of contributed labor among its foundations.
These priorities are incompatible on commercial-use rights. The OSI framework's requirements
are documented in its [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd), particularly
its provisions on redistribution and fields of endeavor.

CTH treats these as independently held philosophies. Neither is treated here as subordinate
to the other. CTH does not recognize OSI as an umbrella authority over its own definition,
seek its validation, or define its identity through opposition to it. Naming the distinction
helps readers understand the terms; it does not establish a hierarchy of legitimacy.

## Values and legal implementation

CTH currently publishes PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0. Its philosophical commitments
are broader than what that text alone implements. A separate commercial-services instrument
is proposed to express conditional services permission without rewriting
the standard PolyForm text. Rights-holder authority, third-party compatibility, and enforcement
remain legal questions, not conclusions supplied by this PRN.

The project should communicate both its commitments and the limits of their implementation
plainly. A statement of intent must not be presented as an already effective grant or duty.

## Amendment

Revisions must increment the version and date, preserve the distinction between philosophy
and legal terms, and accurately describe the status of any commercial permission. Changes
here cannot create license rights or override INV, DOM, FEAT, or incorporated SPEC contracts.
